Web App · Live Music & Maps

Giggity — finding London gigs

I kept hearing about gigs after they had happened or sold out. Checking individual venue websites wasn’t working, so I started building somewhere to bring the listings together.

Giggity collects events from Ticketmaster and selected London venue programmes, tracks changes and puts them into a searchable list and venue map. What began as a personal annoyance has become a way to learn the engineering around the data: building a web interface, testing the collection process and getting the whole thing online.

Under the bonnet · Giggity

Giggity — under the bonnet

The first problem was finding the gigs. The next was working out how to bring together listings that don’t all describe the same event in the same way.

A Python pipeline collects and checks the data. SQLite holds the current events and changes, and a SvelteKit interface makes them searchable and easy to browse. Reviewed data is published separately, so updating the listings doesn’t mean rebuilding the whole application.

The analytical work was familiar territory. Building the interface, connecting the pieces and deploying it gave me plenty of new things to learn.

Under the bonnet · TransitFlow

TransitFlow — under the bonnet

TransitFlow started as a fun way to return to network modelling. I wanted to see whether I could turn metro data into a model I could explore, then repeat the process for another city.

Beijing is the starting point. The project brings together station connections, geographic data and simulated services to explore the network and its interchanges. It draws on the spatial-network thinking from my PhD research, with the added challenge of building something interactive.
